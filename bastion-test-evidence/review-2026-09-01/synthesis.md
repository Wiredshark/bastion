## 2. OWNER JUDGEMENTS

### Population contraction
No Contract arm exists; an over-capacity town currently just freezes.
- **A. Manor Lords shape (recommended default):** gate emigration off the same mood/approval signal that already gates Grow, symmetric, rate-bounded (their reference: -1 household/month under a floor, reversing automatically above it), shedding by household per HOUSING IS BEDS. *Tradeoff:* reuses existing bed/household accounting; Song of Syx explicitly walked back per-citizen emigration in favor of an unrest response, so this is not a safe default to assume correct without the matched-control test below.
- **B. Dwarf Fortress shape (no exit valve):** unhappiness converts to internal dysfunction (route into the existing Defend/unrest machinery) rather than population loss. *Tradeoff:* no new death/departure plumbing at all; never actually relieves an over-capacity town, just makes it visibly worse in place.
- **C. Frostpunk shape (upstream only):** control capacity by refusing arrivals before over-capacity happens, never shed the existing roster. *Tradeoff:* doesn't fix an ALREADY over-capacity town (the 46-in-10-houses case that motivated the question) — solves the next town, not this one.
**What would falsify a mechanism, whichever is picked:** build the over-capacity fixture (roster > house capacity, mood held under the departure floor), run N days; if departures don't track the trigger — a happy-but-overcrowded town empties, or a miserable-but-under-capacity town never sheds — it's wired to the wrong condition and needs re-deriving.

### Hunger consequences
Hunger currently cannot kill (downed=0 across 4,867 samples).
- **A. RimWorld ladder (highest cost, best fit to existing architecture):** a second `malnutrition` stat that only accumulates once hunger sits at 0, staged incapacitation → death, fully reversible until the last stage. Reuses the existing mood-shortfall/Despond shape almost exactly.
- **B. Banished blunt roll (cheapest, riskiest):** flat per-tick death chance once hunger has sat at 0 past a grace window, no intermediate stage. *Risk:* compounds badly with EatFrom job-pipeline latency that isn't real scarcity — must be measured first (see falsifier).
- **C. Sims/Frostpunk-style incapacitation, no death:** reuse Despond's own machinery — a loud, costly, non-lethal "Starving" preempting job. Smallest lift, no death/corpse/population plumbing needed at all, but never delivers survival-game stakes.
**What would falsify any death/incapacitation gate before it ships:** measure how much of a colonist's time at hunger==0 in a normally-supplied colony is real scarcity vs. EatFrom pipeline latency. If colonists routinely sit at 0 as a queuing artifact, any threshold timed off raw hunger fires on well-fed colonies — the sustained-zero window must be calibrated against measured latency, not assumed.

### Autofound siting vs. the flat ruling
The flat gate is a Ben ruling already (e22c9a1d37: "NOT SHIPPED: relaxing the flat gate would override a Ben ruling").
- **A. Status quo — binary flat veto (current):** simple, predictable, matches "flat wins among adequate sites," but a marginal 2-house/1-field flat site can beat a 10-house/2-field site purely on flatness.
- **B. Civ5/DF two-stage (floor, then weighted sum):** hard minimum-capacity floor first (a site must clear a houses/fields threshold to even be a candidate), then score survivors by an additive weighted sum where flatness is one term among several, never a sole gate. *Cost:* requires picking weights (a further judgment call) and separately verifying `alt_range` is sampled at the site's own local footprint, not a coarser grain — DF's own bug tracker documents exactly this confound (region-level elevation stats poisoning locally-flat sites).
- **C. RimWorld — don't fuse the axes at all:** surface separate top-N lists (flattest-adequate vs. highest-capacity), let the picker (autofound heuristic or Ben) choose, sidestepping the domination problem.
**What would falsify B specifically:** re-run the 42-site/16,384-block search under floor+weighted-sum with non-degenerate weights; if the 10-house/2-field town still can't beat the 2-house flat site under ANY weighting that leaves flatness meaningfully influential, the defect isn't the scoring shape — it's upstream (candidate-generation truncation, or `alt_range` sampled at the wrong grain), and that's what needs fixing instead.

## Why two records

The spec's audit basis (`5de5361bc53cdac252c30c43cc979512550ae5e9`,
`bastion/block-B6HAUL`) is on a diverged history from the actual target
(`bastion/apex` = `bastion/det-fixtures` tip). But the *audit-side* delta
between the merge-base and the spec's audit basis
(`927c2063a3f587007988d427f7b924b4250b13c3` → `5de5361bc`) is exactly 27
paths, all documentation, VM-runner scripts, or GCP billing tooling — **zero
production source**. So the spec's audited *production* tree is
byte-identical at the merge-base, and the merge-base *is* an ancestor of the
target. Two separate, honest questions, two separate records:

1. **Formal**: does the target descend from the exact commit the spec
   claims it audited? No — `BLOCK-DIVERGED-HISTORY`.
2. **Effective**: does the target descend from the commit where the spec's
   *production* observations were actually taken? Yes — but that commit
   (927c2063a3f) was never given production-impact policy rules, so the
   233-path effective delta correctly fails closed rather than being
   assumed safe.

`BLOCK-UNKNOWN-IMPACT`, not `READMIT-PRODUCTION`: the admission tool's
impact policy (`readme/APEX-SOURCE-IMPACT-POLICY-v1.toml`) only has three
exact documentation-path rules. It has no rules classifying the 217 other
changed paths, so per its fail-closed design (section 5.1 of the A.1
schema) it correctly reports `UnknownImpact` rather than guessing they're
all `ProductionOrBuild`. This is the intended handoff: **APEX-A.2 is the
targeted production re-audit** this pair of records calls for — it was
already built (readme/apex/APEX-FINDING-STATUS-MATRIX-v1.csv) directly
against current source rather than against either admission basis.

Both `target/apex-source-admission-*/source-admission-v1.json` files are
disposable evidence per A.1's own rule and are not committed; the digests
above are the durable record.

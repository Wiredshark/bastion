## B5.6b — DESIGNED (the z-extent model, decided; build as its own block)
**The data model (this was the blocker — decisions):**
- Designations gain an explicit **`z_extent { down: u16, up: u16 }` relative to the painted surface**,
  replacing the hardcoded `min.z-2`. Defaults preserve current semantics exactly (existing designation
  types get their current effective depths as explicit defaults — zero behavior change until UI sets them).
- **Per-cell surface-relative resolution:** painted footprint + per-cell terrain surface sample + z_extent
  resolves to the claim volume (job-cell generation stays per-block as today). v1 volumes are simple:
  footprint × depth-from-surface, staged level-by-level (§3v's paint-footprint-set-depth model). Full
  terrain-sculpted volumes are NOT v1.
- **Zone taxonomy alignment:** thin zones (stockpile/chop) default down=0–2; the mine designation is the
  first real consumer of user-set depth. Same z_extent field §3v's mine framework and §3w's boundary
  consumers expect — one schema, locked here.
**Then the rendering + UX (buildable once the model exists):**
- Conformed fills + volumetric rendering via a new DebugShape variant carrying pre-conformed geometry,
  built by a helper WITH terrain access (this helper = the reusable overlay-renderer utility; the §3w
  boundary overlay is its next customer). Low-alpha fill, stronger edges, depth rings every N levels,
  Z-slice clipping.
- Volume-selection UX: drag footprint → scroll/hold-drag sets depth with live ring preview + level count;
  precision numeric field on the tool; both paths synced, producing identical zones.
Gate: 6-deep mine zone on a slope reads correctly (god-cam/side/mid-excavation); both selection paths
identical; slice clips sanely. Tag `bastion-block-B5.6b`.

# B5.6 — Zone/Selection Visuals: Terrain Draping, Volume Highlight, Visibility Toggle (patch block)

> **For Ben:** paste into a game-build session at `E:\veloren-master` (or queue after B5.5). Small,

## WHY
The live demo showed the painted-zone outline rendering as a flat rectangle floating over 3D terrain — you
can't judge what a zone covers on a hillside. And zones are becoming volumetric (mine zones with z-extent,
future-work §3v), so "what does this zone contain" needs to be *visible*. Finally: designation visuals
should be dismissible — sometimes you just want to watch the colony.

## Part 1 — Selection & zone outlines DRAPE the terrain
- **Evidence:** `readme/evidence-b56-floating-selection-bleabrolm.png` — live screenshot of zone outlines
  hovering flat in mid-air over sloped terrain. This is the bug, photographed.
- **Diagnostic lead from Ben:** toggling the Z-slice (F9) and back FIXED the floating in that session —
  strongly suggests the overlay is rendering at the slice-plane height (or a stale cached height) instead
  of terrain surface height. Investigate this first; the draping work below supersedes the bug either way,
  but the overlay must be verified correct across ALL slice modes and after slice toggles.
- The zone/designation boundary line follows the ground: sample terrain surface height along the outline
  path and render the line conformed to it — over the bump, down the dip, hugging the actual surface
  (small constant hover offset so it never z-fights). Same treatment for the in-progress selection
  rectangle WHILE dragging (live draping, not just after commit).
- Zone FILL overlays (the tinted area) likewise conform per-cell to the terrain surface height.
- Build the draping as a small reusable overlay-renderer utility — the colony-boundary overlay (§3w) and
  future map overlays will reuse it (build-once).
- **Test:** paint across a hill and a pit; screenshot; the outline visibly follows both. Toggle Z-slice
  through all modes mid-paint and after commit — draping stays correct. No z-fighting, no floating
  segments, acceptable cost with many zones visible.

## Part 1b — Smarter erasing (type filters + area delete)
- **Erase-by-type:** the Erase tool gains a designation-type filter (All / Mine / Chop / Build / Stockpile /
  …): a small selector on the tool (cycled or clicked) so erasing a chop zone overlapping a mine zone
  doesn't nuke both. Default: All (current behavior).
- **Area-erase:** drag a rectangle with Erase → all designations (of the filtered type) within it are
  removed in one action — the bulk complement to B5.5's paint-erase and per-zone radial Delete. Same clean
  claim-release rules; same one-cycle release assertion.
- **Test:** overlapping Mine+Chop zones; filter=Chop; paint-erase and area-erase remove only Chop; claims
  release cleanly; Mine untouched.

## Part 2 — VOLUMETRIC zones render their volume (+ how you SELECT a volume)
- Zones with z-extent (deep mine zones now; tall building zones later) render as a translucent highlighted
  VOLUME, not just a surface patch: visible top face + subtle side walls down to the zone's depth, and —
  where terrain has been dug — the remaining designated volume readable inside the excavation (you can see
  how deep the mine zone goes and what's left to dig).
- **Volume selection UX (two paths, both):**
  - **Drag-in-space:** drag the footprint on the ground as today, then set depth directly — scroll wheel
    (or hold+vertical-drag) extends the volume down/up level by level, with the depth rings and a live
    "N levels" count rendering AS you adjust. Confirm to commit.
  - **Precision panel:** a small numeric field on the tool (or in the radial) to type/step the exact
    z-extent ("8 levels", or "to depth Z") — for when eyeballing isn't enough. The two stay in sync
    (drag sets the number; the number drives the preview).
- Keep it legible, not loud: low-alpha fill + slightly stronger edges; depth rings/level lines every N
  z-levels so depth is *countable* at a glance. Respect the existing Z-slice view (volume clips correctly
  when slicing).
- Surface-only zones (stockpiles etc.) keep the flat draped fill — volume rendering only when z_extent > 1.
- **Test:** a mine zone 6 levels deep on a slope: volume reads correctly from god-cam, side view, and
  mid-excavation; both selection paths produce identical zones; z-slice clips it sanely.

## Part 3 — The visuals TOGGLE (enjoy the colony)
- One keybind + settings entry: **Designation Visuals: ON / SUBTLE / OFF.**
  - ON = full outlines + fills + volumes (working mode).
  - SUBTLE = thin outlines only, no fills/volumes (situational awareness without clutter).
  - OFF = nothing rendered (pure colony-watching; designations remain fully active — this is VISUAL only).
- Painting/erasing input while OFF: either auto-bump to ON while the tool is active (preferred — you can
  always see what you're painting) then restore the chosen mode, or block with a hint. Pick the former
  unless it fights the input-context system.
- **Test:** toggle cycles cleanly; OFF hides everything; painting while OFF auto-reveals; colonists keep
  working regardless (visual-only, zero sim impact — assert B4/B5 scenarios green with visuals OFF).

## Part 4 — Pile visual SCALING (piles grow with count, cap at a ceiling)
The B5.5 piles need to look right at any count — a pile of 400 stone rendering as three pebbles looks odd:
- **The pile mesh grows with its count** through visual tiers — e.g. scatter (1–5) → small heap (6–20) →
  heap (21–60) → large mound (61–150) → capped great-mound (150+, stops growing; the count keeps rising,
  the visual plateaus). Tier thresholds per item type where sensible (a "pile" of weapons vs stone reads
  differently — a simple per-type mesh set is fine, generic heap acceptable v1).
- Smooth-ish: tier transitions on merge/draw shouldn't pop distractingly (a brief scale-lerp is plenty).
- The pile's count remains inspectable (existing tooltip/inspect path shows `stone ×247`).
- **Test:** mine a large area → watch a pile walk up the tiers and plateau at cap; draw it down → tiers
  descend; conservation stays exact throughout (visual tier changes NEVER touch the count).
